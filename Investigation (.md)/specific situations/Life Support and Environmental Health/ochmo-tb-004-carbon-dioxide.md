## Background

CO -Induced Headaches 2 Research and past historical data has revealed an association between reported headaches and CO levels 2 among crewmembers.

- A study of data collected from Expeditions 2 to 31 (N=49) found that CO level, age at launch, time in-flight,

2 and data source were significantly associated with reported headaches on the ISS.

- 19 of the 49 astronauts reported experiencing headaches (~38.7%).

- To keep the risk of headache below 1%, average 7-day CO needs to be maintained below 2.5 mmHg (current

2

- ISS range: 1 to 9 mmHg; 3001 standard requires <3 mmHg).

- Most experienced headaches were moderate intensity, requiring the use of analgesics in many cases.

- A retrospective analysis of the case reports found a total of 46 days with reported headaches and 1,670 days

of non-reports (no headaches observed).

- Based on this data, the current NASA-STD-3001 Volume 2 Rev D [V2 6004] limits the average 1-hour CO

2 partial pressure (ppCO ) in the habitat volume to no more than 3 mmHg to keep incidence of headaches 2 below 1.4%.

### CO Washout System

2

- Carbon Dioxide washout describes the process of removing CO in an enclosed space. For the purpose

2 of this brief, we are discussing CO washout in a spacesuit and space helmet. 2

- CO is a significant concern as the consequence of inadequate CO removal can result in hypercapnia

2 2 and related symptoms ranging from headache and fatigue to impaired cognitive function and death.

- CO removal in a space helmet is challenging as it is difficult to ensure the quantity of CO that is

2 2 removed from the suit and not forming pockets in any area of the suit or helmet. It is also important to ensure when CO is purged, it does not disrupt the critical breathable oxygen balance. 2

- Fresh air outlet from the PLSS is located at the rear base of the helmet. Air flow is directed from the

back of the crewmember’s head over their head and face into the suit where it exits the suit and flows into the PLSS for filtration. This provides a continuous airflow, so the exhaled CO is entrained with the 2 fresh air loop from the PLSS. This mixed air then flows down into the suit to where it exits the suit and flows back into the PLSS.

- Exhaled air including CO is flowed through an adsorbent charcoal bed and contamination control

2 cartridges composed or either LiOH or METOX. This removes CO , trace gases, and odors. The LiOH 2 cartridges are stored on the vehicle and replaced on the ground, the METOX cartridges can be regenerated on orbit. Either of these are installed in the PLSS prior to EVA.
