## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Appendicitis and cholecystitis are two relatively common conditions that can occur in otherwise healthy people such as astronauts. It has been suggested that prophylactic surgery before spaceflight may be useful to drive down the risk of these conditions in astronauts on deep space missions. However, a history of prior abdominal surgery is the single biggest risk for subsequent small bowel obstruction (SBO). The Integrated Medical Model (IMM) was used to compare SBO risk under each set of conditions, no surgery versus prophylactic surgery. The results show that there is no benefit to prophylactic surgery in reducing adverse outcomes associated with SBO. Further, prophylactic laparoscopic appendectomy may confer a higher risk of EVAC versus no surgery, according to the model.

## BACKGROUND

Appendicitis and cholecystitis are two relatively common conditions that can occur in otherwise healthy people such as astronauts. These conditions are generally treated by laparoscopic surgical removal of the offending organ. It has been suggested that prophylactic removal of the appendix and gall bladder would eliminate the risk of these conditions in astronauts on deep space missions [5]. However, a history of prior abdominal surgery is the single biggest risk for subsequent small bowel obstruction (SBO) [6]. SBO is a serious and life threatening medical condition that is also often managed surgically. The trade off between prophylactic laparoscopic surgery to prevent appendicitis and cholecystis, and the future risk of small bowel obstruction (SBO) due to abdominal surgery are not known. ### Model Background

The IMM is a stochastic model that uses Monte Carlo methodology to simulate medical events and outcomes during spaceflight missions. The IMM consists of a three-step process. First, mission and crewmember characteristics are defined to create a mission profile(s) or design reference mission (DRM). Next, medical events and outcomes during each mission are randomly generated based on pre-defined values and probability distributions. Finally, total crew health and mission impact outcomes are summarized. For this report the primary outcomes of interest are evacuation (EVAC) and loss of crew life (LOCL). The IMM is most useful for comparing risks across multiple DRMs within the same service request. Biased results may be incurred due to limitations of the model, but the bias will be consistent across mission profiles within the same service request.

### Cohort Incidence Data

A literature search was performed to find suitable cohort data that defines the risk of SBO after appendectomy and cholecystectomy. A very large population registry study, Angenete, E. et al. “Effect of Laparoscopy on the Risk of Small-Bowel Obstruction” [6], was found that broke down risk of subsequent SBO by pathology and surgical methodology (open versus laparascopic) over the five year study period. If prophylactic surgery were to be performed on astronuats it would likely be by lararoscopy because this method is the least invasive and is generally considered the standard of care for both appendectomy and cholecystectomy, thus data for uncomplicated laparascopic surgery in persons with no prior history of surgery were used.

For the control case the risk of SBO was assumed to be zero because no abdominal surgery had been performed. Strictly speaking the actual risk of SBO without a prior history of abdominal surgery is not zero, but it is a very small number and occurs in otherwise unhealthy or ill persons – a population very unlike the astronaut corps. ### Medical Conditions and Resources

The incidence values of IMM medical conditions occurring during simulated missions are based on historical mission and cohort data. For each condition, a defined set of medical resources are used to treat the condition. This in-flight medical treatment is assumed to follow a specified protocol [8] for each condition and is constrained by resource availability on the ISS. In this report, the fully treated scenario was used to account for the assumptions and limitations of the IMM v3 (stated below).

### Main Outcomes

The IMM primary medical outcomes for this report include crew evacuation (EVAC) and loss of crew life (LOCL). EVAC in the context of IMM means that medical evacuation from the ISS would be considered for definitive treatment of the afflicted crew member(s). EVAC is considered an end state result if any of the following criteria is met: 1) potential LOCL; 2) potential significant permanent impairment; or 3) potential intractable pain. When EVAC is calculated by the IMM, no consideration has been given to the availability of a return vehicle or the likelihood of a successful clinical outcome should a return vehicle be available. LOCL in the context of IMM should be interpreted to mean that the clinical scenario resulted in death of the affected crew member(s). These assumptions should result in slightly high (conservative) IMM forecasts for the probabilities of EVAC and LOCL.

### Modeling Approach

One hundred thousand (100,000) trials were run for each DRM; each trial can be 5 01 JUNE 2017

This ensures that the distribution of outcomes meets convergence criteria. Convergence criteria are met when there is less than, or equal to a 5% change in the standard deviation of primary model outcomes.

EVAC and LOCL are calculated as probabilities. Ninty-five percent confidence intervals (CI) for EVAC and LOCL were obtained using joint parameter bootstrap resampling of the simulation output using percentiles. Statistical significance was assessed using 95% CI to test differences between compared outcomes.

### IMM ASSUMPTIONS AND LIMITATIONS

1. The IMM is baselined to the ISS including medical capabilities and environment.

This includes low earth orbit and vehicle ambient atmosphere equal to approximately 14.7 psi terrestrial ambient atmosphere, and not to a Mars mission environment.

2. All medical conditions are correctly diagnosed and all treatments are 100%

effective.

3. The IMM v3 does not include a mission timeline, all conditions occur at launch on

day one. This includes conditions known to have a delayed onset that would occur outside the mission duration (e.g. Visual Impairment due to Increased Intracranial Pressure).

4. Medical conditions are independent of each other; i) a mission could end in more

than one evacuation or loss of crew life being considered for the same crew member, and ii) additional medical conditions can occur in a crew member assigned an end state of EVAC or LOCL.

5. IMM v3 does not consider partial treatment or use of alternative medications.

6. IMM source data in the iMED database undergo periodic updates to ensure best

alignment to the real world system. 6 01 JUNE 2017
