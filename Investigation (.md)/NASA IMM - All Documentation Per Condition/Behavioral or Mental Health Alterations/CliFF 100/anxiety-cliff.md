## Branch Baseline

Anxiety and depression are the two most common mental health problems seen in the general medical setting. The four most common anxiety disorders (excluding simple phobias that seldom present clinically) are generalized anxiety disorder, panic disorder, social anxiety disorder, and posttraumatic stress disorderIn space anxiety may manifest as symptoms considered within the range of a normal response to circumstances, or may meet the criteria for a disorder. The differential diagnosis for anxiety on orbit includes other mental disorders, withdrawal from certain medications, exposure to toxins, endocrine dysfunction or other medical illnesses. (Kroenke, 2007)

In the US space program there have been a total of 37 events reported in flight all in the range of normal response to mild symptoms that were short lived. (Walton, 2010). </

## Incidence Data Included in the Model

The model imports the following incidence information from the IMM database: incidence data category, space adaption status, incidence data, incidence and occurrence distribution data and incidence data characteristics. Data category defines the type of data available for incidence calculations (i.e. raw data vs. fixed data). Space adaptation identifies medical events that onset occurs in the first 5 days of flight and does not reoccur. Incidence values vary by data category and define the number of medical events per person-year or medical events per person-at-risk for each medical condition. Incidence values can be modified by crew characteristics such as gender. Modifying characteristics are noted below and incidence values are listed individually for each characteristic. Distributions are assigned to medical events incidence values and occurrences in the model. For each crew member in a given trial, the incidence and number of occurrences of each medical condition are randomly selected from these probability distributions. Detailed descriptions of the distributions are included in the IMM Technical Description Document.

## Incidence Comments

Prevalence studies in psychiatric epidemiology outnumber incidence investigations. The Stirling County Study is a longitudinal investigation based on repeated cross-sectional surveys and cohort follow up investigations carried out between 1952 and 1970. For a diagnosis of anxiety, the diagnostic algorithm requires a positive evidence of typical symptoms of autonomic hyperactivity and motor tension in conjunction with apprehension, especially apprehension about having a nervous breakdown; it causes impairment in one’s ordinary role, and persists over a minimum direction of one month. The incidence rates for anxiety, for the age 40-49 cohort are 1.9 for male, and 7.1 for female. Both per 1,000 per year (Murphy, 1988) Rates per person year are 0.0019 for male and 0.0071 for female.

A study to determine work related and personal factors associated with occupational stress in submariners surveyed a random stratified sample of UK Naval service personnel. In total, 116 questionnaires from a total of 219 were returned (a 53% response rate). Of the 116 questionnaires returned, 105 were fully completed; therefore, this was the sample used in the remainder of the analyses. A total of 51% of the respondents were married and the average age was 34 years (range: 19–50 years, SD 7.9 years). In total, 40% of submariners in the 2007 sample were found to be suffering from stress. The findings showed that stress rates in submariners were stable across time, similar to the general Royal Navy. This is in direct contrast to a report issued by the Health and Safety Executive, which states that stress is an increasing problem in the UK.

While the stress rate in submariners at 40% is considerably higher than that of the general British population, it does not appear to be increasing. The main personal and demographic factors associated with stress in submariners were low organizational commitment, over- commitment to role, emotional lability and age. The main occupational factors associated with higher stress levels were physical working conditions aboard submarines, lack of peer support and lack of leader support. For submariners, and possibly for other groups working in isolated and extreme environments, the constraints of a restricted environment introduce additional factors that can be associated with stress. ### Treatment and Outcomes

Based on the best evidence available best and worst case percentage ranges are assigned. The medical condition is divided into three clinical phases in all three clinical phases a functional impairment is assigned. The clinical phase I and II’s functional impairments remain for the duration for that respective clinical phase. The function impairment in clinical phase III represents final impairment for this medical condition and remains for the rest of the mission. Other mission end states include evacuation and loss of crew life.

The IMM checks medical resource availability once the number of medical events and scenarios has been established. If there is not sufficient essential resources called out in the CliFF, then the medical condition will go down an untreated best or worst case scenario pathway.

### Scenario Comments

Best case scenario is defined as anxiety that resolves spontaneously or requires minimal pharmacological therapy or psychological counseling. Worst case scenario is defined as severe anxiety that requires prolonged pharmacologic therapy and/or psychological counseling.

Percentages of worst versus best case scenario were estimated using terrestrial data. Epidemiology studies in Canada have broken out the prevalence rate of anxiety disorders by severity and suggest that 9.2% of the population will suffer from mild anxiety disorders, 2.2% will suffer from chronic, and 1.2% will suffer from serious conditions with significant disability. (Provincial Strategy Advisory Committee for Anxiety Disorders, 2002) All 3 severity percentages were added (16.6) and 1.2% of severe disorders resulted in 9.5% of worst case scenario. To allow for uncertainty the worst case is estimated at 0-9%, thus the best case scenario is 91-100%. 5/11/2020 4:51:15 PM Anxiety Cliff page: 4

### Best Case Comments

The functional impairments (FI) calculation for Anxiety follows the IMM mild non-space adaptation template. Description of symptoms is based on Anxiety Table p. 370. Ratings are based on Impairment Score of Brief Psychiatric Rating Scale (BPRS) Table 14-9, p. 357. (American Medical Association, 2008) By definition in Clinical Phase I, FI is 100%. Duration of physical exam as described in ISS checklist for the best case scenario is estimated as 15 minutes. During Clinical Phase II, FI is estimated in 0 to 10%. Duration is estimated as 0 to 72 hours, In Clinical Phase III, full recovery is expected and FI is 0%. By definition EVAC and LOCL are not expected for the best case scenario.

### Worst Case Comments

The functional impairments (FI) calculation for Anxiety follows the IMM mild non-space adaptation template. Description of symptoms is based on Anxiety Table p. 370. Ratings are based on Impairment Score of Brief Psychiatric Rating Scale (BPRS) Table 14-9, p. 357. (American Medical Association, 2008) By definition in Clinical Phase I, FI is 100%. Duration of physical exam and medication as described in ISS checklist is estimated as 30 minutes.

During Clinical Phase II, FI is estimated in 5 to 20%. Duration is estimated in 120 hours to 4320 (5 days to 6 months)(Bandelow, 2012). In Clinical Phase III, full recovery may or may not occur and FI is estimated at 0-10%. EVAC has occurred in analog populations. (Goodman, 2011)) To allow for uncertainty EVAC is estimated at 0-5%. By definition, LOCL is not expected for this condition.
