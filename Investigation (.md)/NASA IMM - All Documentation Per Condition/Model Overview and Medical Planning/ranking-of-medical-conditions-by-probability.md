### Ranking of Medical Conditions by Probability

## Background

This Data Request Report was prepared using information solely from iMED v5.0 on October 23, 2014.

The iMED contains data from multiple references including terrestrial, analog, and in-flight sources, as well as incidence value estimates from event-driven numerical simulations when space and terrestrial data are insufficient. While a complete description of assumptions and limitations associated with the data contained in the iMED is documented (2) and available on request, the list below highlights assumptions and limitations pertinent to this report:

- Terrestrial and analog data are an approximation of the data expected from the medically flight-

qualified astronaut corps.

- In-flight data are only from United States (US) National Aeronautics and Space Administration

(NASA) crew members from Apollo, Skylab, Mir, Space Transportation System (STS, i.e.

Shuttle), and International Space Station (ISS) Expedition (Exp) missions.

- In-flight data are known to represent a sampling of information from published and/or available

sources (i.e. they are incomplete); Shuttle data are primarily from STS-1 through STS-114; ISS data are primarily from Exp 1-13.

- If a space adaptation condition occurs during a mission for a given astronaut, it occurs only once.

- iMED medical data and resources are baselined to the ISS environment, which includes low earth

orbit, no gravity transitions, and vehicle ambient atmosphere equal to approximately 14.7 psi terrestrial ambient atmosphere. (During exploration missions, this has potential impact to the incidence values for the following medical conditions: including Acute Radiation Syndrome, Altitude Sickness, Barotrauma, Decompression Sickness Secondary to Extravehicular Activity (EVA), bone fracture conditions, as well as potentially space adaptation conditions.)

- Incidence rates of Burns Secondary to Fire, Smoke Inhalation, and Toxic Exposure: Ammonia are

derived from the ISS Fire Model (i.e. an event-driven numerical simulation) versus actual in- flight events. (This is an import consideration for putting these data in context.)

All crew members were assigned coronary artery calcium (CAC) scores of zero. None of the crew members had crowns, wore contacts, or had a history of abdominal surgery. For each of the DRMs above, the probability of at least one event occurring was calculated for each of the applicable ninety-eight of the one hundred listed medical conditions (Appendix A) using condition-specific incidence data from iMED and mission-specific parameters. The non-applicable medical conditions were Small Bowel Obstruction and Dental: Crown Loss.

## Summary

Ninety-eight medical conditions were ranked in order of probability that at least one event occurs during a specified DRM. Six DRMs were examined with a single male or single female crew member, resulting in twelve DRMs assessed (Table 1). DRMs ranged in duration from one to thirty-six months. For shorter duration DRMs, more SA medical conditions were ranked in the top ten conditions listed. With the exception of urinary tract infection in the female DRMs, the top ten conditions and probability values were identical in male versus female DRMs, and all top ten conditions listed for all DRMs have occurred previously in-flight. Of note, the top ten medical conditions and probability values for a twelve month Deep Space journey and habitation mission with 5 EVAs (Table 6) are identical to those of the twelve month Low Earth Orbit mission with 2 EVAs (Table 3) for both the male and female DRMs, respectively.

The similarities between the top ten medical conditions and probability values in both DRMs are a result of identical mission length having a greater parameter influence than other mission parameter differences given the current assumptions and limitations. Most of the reported conditions in these two mission scenarios are generated based on time (Tables 3 and 6). These results highlight the importance of ensuring that the assumptions and limitations provided are used to provide appropriate context for interpreting these data. 20 2 FEBRUARY 2015
