# Microgravity CPR

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7607644/

Reference status: Training summary of DGLRM/ESAM 2020 and ERC 2025. Use the approved procedure for the vehicle and crew.

Scenario terms: CPR | RCP | cardiac arrest | paro cardiaco | cardiopulmonary resuscitation | reanimacion cardiopulmonar | not breathing normally | only gasping | no respira | solo jadea

## Onboard response

### Phase 1. Immediate basic life support at the scene

1. **Check responsiveness and call for help.** Check whether the crewmate responds and is breathing normally. Alert the crew and activate the onboard emergency checklist. Ask another crewmember to coordinate with ground medical support if communications and staffing permit.
2. **Establish support while floating.** If the crewmate is unresponsive and is not breathing normally or is only gasping, establish stable support using the technique practiced for the mission. With Evetts–Russomano, if trained for it, the rescuer braces against the casualty by looping one leg over a shoulder and the other around the torso and locking the ankles behind the casualty.
3. **Begin chest compressions at the scene.** When CPR is indicated, compress the center of the chest at 100–120 per minute, aiming for 5–6 cm in an adult and allowing the chest to recoil. Do not delay compressions to reach the medical station.
4. **Transfer with help.** When additional crew arrive, coordinate movement to the ship’s medical restraint system, such as the CMRS on the ISS, with as little interruption to compressions as possible.

### Phase 2. Advanced care with equipment and restraint

1. **Secure the casualty and use the AED.** Fasten the casualty to the approved, electrically isolated restraint surface before applying the defibrillator. Use the ship’s AED if available, clear contact for analysis and shock, and follow its prompts. Resume compressions promptly after a shock.
2. **Continue compressions with approved support.** A trained rescuer can use the handstand method if the checklist allows it and the cabin geometry permits bracing the feet against the opposite surface. Use a mechanical compressor only if the equipment is approved, available and the crew can install it without delaying compressions.
3. **Assign airway management.** Once the casualty is secured and at least two trained rescuers are available, have the medical crew follow the onboard airway checklist. A supraglottic airway is an option when the checklist and training support it.
4. **Coordinate ventilation with compressions.** Trained crew using a bag-mask before an advanced airway give 2 breaths for every 30 compressions. With an advanced airway in place, trained providers give 1 breath every 6 seconds during continuous compressions.
5. **Rotate rescuers.** When another trained compressor is available, change about every 2 minutes or sooner if compressions are deteriorating. Keep the pause as short as possible.

## Technique notes

- The 2020 DGLRM/ESAM guidance describes Evetts-Russomano as an initial technique that lets the rescuer stabilize against a free-floating patient. It discusses the handstand technique at the medical station when the cabin geometry, rescuer and approved procedure permit it.
- The 2025 ERC concludes that available evidence does not establish a preferred microgravity compression technique. Use the technique the crew has trained for. Secure the patient before defibrillation, advanced airway management or vascular access.
- DGLRM/ESAM discusses advanced airway management after restraint and with at least two rescuers. Use the approved airway procedure and the equipment the crew has trained with.
- Adult BLS training targets in AHA 2025 include 100–120 compressions per minute and approximately 5–6 cm of depth. When two or more rescuers are available, the AHA describes a compressor change about every two minutes. The mission checklist governs the actual procedure, including ventilation, airway devices and backup methods.
- An alert or report of chest pain or suspected heart attack alone does not establish a need for CPR. Reassess responsiveness and breathing and use the cardiac arrest pathway if its criteria are met.
