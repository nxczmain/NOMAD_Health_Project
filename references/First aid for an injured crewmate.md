# First aid for an injured crewmate

Source: https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/articles/the-three-cs-of-first-aid-check-call-care

These public first-aid references are for training. On a mission, use the approved emergency procedure and reach the crew medical officer. Give responders the location, observed symptoms, and any change in breathing or responsiveness.

## Coughing up blood or severe bleeding

Coughing up blood with breathing difficulty, chest or upper back pain, or more than a few spots or streaks calls for emergency help. Report the amount you can see, any external wound, and whether the person is breathing normally and responding. Stay with them if safe and follow the crew medical officer or emergency responder. For a separate severe external wound, apply firm direct pressure with a dressing if trained and safe. That step is for a visible external wound.

Sources: https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/coughing-up-blood/ and https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/bleeding-life-threatening-external

## Suspected fracture of a hand, wrist, or limb

Stop the activity and contact the crew medical officer. Help the person keep the injured area at rest in the position found. Avoid trying to straighten the limb or push a protruding bone back in. Report severe pain, bleeding, numbness, discoloration, or an open wound to the officer. If severe bleeding or difficulty breathing is present, activate the emergency procedure immediately.

Source: https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/fractures
